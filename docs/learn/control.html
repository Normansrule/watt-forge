Lesson 6 of 7

Control and maximum power point tracking

A converter's duty cycle is a knob. For a solar converter the question is which way to turn it: a panel delivers its maximum power at one particular voltage, and that voltage moves with sunlight, temperature and shade.

PWM: two loops

A pulse-width modulator (PWM) compares a control voltage with a carrier ramp to produce the gate pulses, so the duty cycle follows the control voltage. A fast inner loop (tens of kilohertz bandwidth) adjusts it to hold the panel voltage at a reference. A slow outer loop, the maximum power point tracker (MPPT), moves that reference. In the flagship the inner loop runs in the microcontroller's high-resolution timer and the outer loop is the 1 kHz state machine below.

The panel's power curve

A PV cell is a current source in parallel with a diode. Short-circuited it delivers its photocurrent at zero volts; open-circuited it reaches V_oc at zero current. Power peaks in between at the maximum power point (MPP). A panel is built from substrings, each with a bypass diode. Shade one substring and the curve grows a second hill, so a tracker that only climbs locally can get stuck on the smaller one.

Perturb and observe

Perturb and observe (P&O) is the simplest tracker: nudge the voltage reference, measure the power, and if power fell, reverse direction. It hunts around the peak, so the step size is a trade-off between tracking speed and steady-state wobble. The flagship's tracker adapts the step: it halves after eight nearly-flat steps and doubles after a big change. Against partial shading it starts, and periodically restarts, with a 40-point global sweep, re-sweeping immediately if power suddenly drops by 30 %.

Mode selection and the state machine

Once the tracker picks a panel voltage V_ref, the ratio M = V_out / V_ref decides the mode: buck below 0.97, boost above 1.03, buck-boost in between, with 1 % hysteresis so it doesn't chatter at a boundary. If the MPP sits within 2 % of the battery voltage, the controller stops switching and closes the bidirectional bypass switch. It loses a little tracking accuracy and saves all switching loss. A lookup table generated by the loss model sets the switching frequency for each ratio and power band.
