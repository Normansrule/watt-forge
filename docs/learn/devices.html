Lesson 5 of 7

Devices: silicon, gallium nitride, silicon carbide

Lesson 4 showed which device parameters cost watts: on-resistance (R_ds(on)) for conduction, output charge (Q_oss) and gate charge (Q_g) for switching, and reverse-recovery charge (Q_rr) for hard commutation. The three semiconductor technologies trade these very differently.

The figures of merit

Doubling a die's area halves R_ds(on) but doubles its charges, so a technology is judged by products that are roughly independent of die size: R_ds(on) x Q_g (drive-limited switching) and R_ds(on) x Q_oss (hard-switched capacitance loss). Why GaN wins at 30-100 V

  • No body diode, Q_rr = 0. A silicon MOSFET's body diode stores charge that is swept out at full voltage on every hard turn-on. GaN high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) conduct in reverse through the channel itself, with nothing to recover.
  • About a fifth of the gate charge at the same R_ds(on), so the drivers are smaller and faster and gate loss is negligible.
  • Roughly half the output charge, so the hard turn-on loss Q_oss V f halves.
  • The catch: reverse conduction during dead time drops ~2 V (versus ~0.8 V for a silicon diode). Dead times must therefore be short, a few nanoseconds, and the gate must stay below ~6 V absolute maximum. Dynamic on-resistance and layout parasitics also matter (Jones, Wang & Costinett, IEEE JESTPE 2016).

Where SiC belongs

Silicon carbide's wide bandgap shines at 650-10,000 V, in EV traction, PV strings and grid tie: it blocks huge voltages in a thin drift layer and runs hot. No SiC MOSFET exists near 100 V, and at 30 V a 650 V part pays for voltage it never uses with several times the on-resistance and ten times the gate charge. The device comparison tool puts all three in the same converter.

Monolithic bidirectional GaN switches

A conventional transistor blocks voltage in one direction only; its body diode or reverse channel conducts the other way. Blocking both ways, as an AC switch, a disconnect or a matrix converter needs, normally takes two transistors back to back, doubling resistance and part count. A monolithic bidirectional switch (BDS) uses one GaN channel with two gates (or a common-drain arrangement) to block both polarities.

  • The flagship uses the Innoscience INV100FQ030C (100 V, 3.2 mOhm max) as its input disconnect, blocking reverse polarity and night-time battery back-feed with one channel instead of two, and as the bypass switch.
  • MIT's condensed buck-boost (CoBB) puts bidirectional switches inside a flying-capacitor buck-boost and cuts passive weight 2.85x, measuring 95.4 % buck and 97.5 % boost peak at 880 W, 200-500 V to 270 V, gate drive excluded (Aron et al., IEEE TPEL 2025).
  • Commercial parts as of the last verification: Infineon CoolGaN BDS at 650 V (TOLT) and 40 V (WLCSP), and Innoscience VGaN at 40 V and 100 V. See Sources.
