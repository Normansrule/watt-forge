Safety

Watt Forge is a design and simulation reference. It is not a guide to building and energising hardware, and nothing here replaces training, supervision and proper lab practice.

Why solar and battery hardware is dangerous

  • A panel cannot be switched off. In daylight its terminals are live, up to about 50 V for one 72-cell panel and far more in a string. Covering it does not reliably make it safe.
  • Batteries deliver enormous fault currents. A 48 V lithium pack can deliver hundreds to thousands of amps into a short, welding tools and starting fires. Fuse the battery connection, close to the battery.
  • Capacitors stay charged. The flying and bus capacitors hold their charge after power is removed. Measure before touching.
  • Flying-capacitor converters have a start-up hazard. Before the flying capacitors are precharged to half the bus, a single switch can see the full input voltage. Always bring a three-level stage up slowly, from a current-limited bench supply, with the precharge verified on an oscilloscope.
  • GaN gates are fragile. A few volts of overshoot above the absolute maximum destroys the device, and a destroyed switch can fail short.

What the reference code is and isn't

The C controller and the Verilog modulator document the control logic precisely and are tested in simulation. They are not production firmware: they have no watchdog, no hardware abstraction, no calibration and no certification. Every file is marked "reference, simulate and review before use".

Any physical build is at your own risk.
