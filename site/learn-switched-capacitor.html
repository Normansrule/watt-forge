Switched-capacitor and hybrid converters

Capacitors store energy far more densely than inductors. A switched-capacitor (SC) converter uses only switches and capacitors to divide or multiply a voltage by a fixed ratio. It hides one catch, an intrinsic loss that no small resistance can remove. A small inductor removes it.

Why capacitors: energy density, from this repo's own parts

Take two real parts from the flagship's bill of materials and compare the energy each stores per unit volume at its working point:

The 2:1 charge pump

Intuition. A flying capacitor is charged in series with the output (Vin - Vout across it), then flipped in parallel with the output (Vout across it). In steady state it settles near Vin/2, and the output sits at Vin/2 minus a drop that behaves exactly like a resistor, R_out.

The catch. Every time a capacitor is connected to a different voltage, the charge that equalises them dissipates energy in the loop resistance. The amount depends on the voltage step, not on how small the resistance is. This is the slow-switching limit (SSL). At high frequency the capacitor never gets far from equilibrium, and the loss becomes ordinary I^2R in the switches, the fast-switching limit (FSL).

R_SSL = sum_i a_c,i^2 / (C_i * f)    R_FSL = 2 * sum_i R_i * a_r,i^2    R_out ~ sqrt(R_SSL^2 + R_FSL^2)
a_c, a_r
charge through each capacitor / switch per period, normalised to the output charge (2:1 series-parallel: 1/2 each)
C_i, R_i
capacitances [F] and switch resistances [ohm]
f
switching frequency [Hz]
Worked example. 48 V into a 23.5 V battery through one 10 uF flying capacitor at 100 kHz: R_SSL = 1/(4 x 10 uF x 100 kHz) = 0.25 ohm. The 0.5 V shortfall below Vin/2 drives (24 - 23.5)/0.25 = 2 A, and the loss is 2^2 x 0.25 = 1.0 W, however good the switches are. tests/test_losses.py::test_switched_capacitor_limits (exact periodic solution, cross-checked by brute-force integration)

Source: Seeman & Sanders, IEEE TPEL 2008, DOI 10.1109/TPEL.2007.915182.

Hybrid: add a small inductor and the charge-sharing loss disappears

Intuition. The SSL loss comes from connecting a capacitor straight to a different voltage. Put an inductor in the path and the capacitor charges through a current source instead: charge moves without a voltage step across a resistance, so the loss drops to ordinary conduction. This is "soft charging" (Lei & Pilawa-Podgurski, IEEE TPEL 2015). A hybrid converter keeps the capacitor doing the voltage division and uses a small inductor only to shape the current.

The three-level flying-capacitor leg is the hybrid used by the flagship. Four switches and one flying capacitor replace a half-bridge. The switch node steps between 0, Vbus/2 and Vbus at twice the switching frequency. Each switch blocks only Vbus/2, and the inductor sees half-size steps at double frequency, so ripple falls up to four-fold. At D = 0.5 the node sits at Vbus/2 all the time: the inductor current stops rippling and the leg is a pure, soft-charged 2:1 SC converter. Drag the duty cycle and compare.
